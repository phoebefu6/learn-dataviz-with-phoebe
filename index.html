The six sessions 📊 · one dataset, all the way through

The running dataset is Daybreak's subscription numbers - signups, cancellations, revenue by roast, retention by cohort. The coffee-subscription company from the SQL, system-design, product-design, communication and course-design courses. Same numbers every session, so the only thing that changes is the decision you made about them.

❓ Session 1 · easy

What is the question?

The chart's job decides its form: magnitude, identity, polarity, change, or one headline number. Plus the answer people find hardest to accept, which is that quite often it should not be a chart.

▶ Start here 📐 Session 2 · easy

Choosing the form

Bar, line, dot, heatmap - picked by the job rather than by habit. Sorting, small multiples, pie charts honestly assessed, and the one rule with no exceptions: never a dual axis.

never two y-scales ⚖️ Session 3 · medium

Honesty

The lie-factor bench. Truncated axes, area against length, 3D, gradients and cherry-picked windows - with the distortion computed from the rectangles the chart actually drew.

★ the bench 🎨 Session 4 · medium

Colour that works

The four jobs colour can do, one rule each. Colour-vision deficiency through a real matrix transform, contrast as arithmetic, and why four hues for four bars of the same measure is a mistake.

compute it, do not judge it 🔎 Session 5 · hands-on

Marks and anatomy

Thin marks, recessive scaffolding, direct labels instead of a legend, text in text colours, the hover layer, and the table view that is not a consolation prize.

the grid is not data 📤 Session 6 · hands-on

Publishing it

The same chart in a report, a slide, a phone and print. Dark mode as a second palette rather than an inversion, forced colours, and what a screen reader actually gets.

What is honest here 🧾

Unusually for a course in this estate, almost everything in the signature widget is measured rather than modelled.

ClaimStatus
The lie factorReally measured off the drawn geometry - truncate the axis and the number moves because the rectangles moved. Blunt by design: it compares the largest and smallest item, so a chart can score 1.00 and still mislead by its window or its denominator, which session 3 covers separately
ContrastReally measured. WCAG 2.1, computed on the chart's own text against its own surface
Colour-vision separationA real matrix transform and an OKLab distance - but a simulation of dichromacy, not what a person with that condition sees. Good enough to catch a palette that will fail
The chart paletteThe validated categorical default. Lightness, chroma, CVD separation and normal-vision floor all pass; the contrast warning on two slots is discharged by direct labels, which the chart carries
Daybreak's numbersA teaching dataset. The measurement code is real
